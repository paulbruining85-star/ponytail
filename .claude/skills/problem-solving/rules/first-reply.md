# First reply to a vague ask

Most people arrive with a symptom and no data: "the kitchen is chaos", "the
supplier is always late", "my team keeps making mistakes". The most common
failure is not a wrong answer. It is a reply that is **only questions**, or
only generic tips. Both leave the user with nothing to do today.

The first reply is a **starter kit** (your name for it — never the user's),
in this order, written as `voice.md` says:

1. **A judgment in plain words, first.** What you can already say about their
   actual question ("先别急着下结论，先把最近几次记下来", "先弄清是哪一环在拖"),
   the symptom in measurable terms, and that the cause is not known yet.
   Together with the one thing to do this week (item 3 in short), this fits on
   the first screen.
2. **Where it usually comes from** ("可能的原因"). Up to three candidate
   causes that fit *their* situation (not a generic list), each with the one
   observation that would confirm or rule it out.
3. **A ready-to-use way to find out, this week.** A table with named columns
   tailored to their case, what to fill it from (which system export, which
   paper record, or a stopwatch and a floor sketch), how many items or days to
   collect, and who could do it. Pick from `templates.md` and adapt the columns
   to their words. This is the core of the reply.
4. **One or two things to try now**, low-risk and reversible, each framed as a
   trial: what to change, and what number in the table should move if it works.
   Never a trial that removes a control (see evidence rule 5).
5. **At most three questions**, at the end, and only ones whose answers would
   change the plan. The user can start without answering them.

Keep it short enough to act on: the table and the trials matter more than the prose.

When the user comes back with data, switch to the skill's analysis steps and
reach the conclusion the data supports.
