# Countermeasures matched to evidence

Pick from the row the evidence supports. Every countermeasure needs an owner
and a check: what you will look at, and when, to see whether it worked.

Stronger measures change the work so the error is hard to make or is caught
before it matters. Weaker measures rely on people remembering. Prefer stronger
where practical, but a weaker measure that fits the evidence beats a strong one
that does not.

| Evidence points to | Usually helps | Usually does not help | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slip or lapse** in a well-practised task (attention pulled away) | Checklist or prompt at the point of use; forcing function (the wrong action cannot be completed); separate look-alike items; reduce interruptions at critical steps; independent check for high-consequence steps | Retraining someone who already knew; warnings; "be more careful" | Error or near-miss count on that step over the next weeks; spot observations |
| **Rule-based mistake** (right rule, wrong situation; or a changed rule) | Rewrite or simplify the rule; make the cue that tells situations apart visible; targeted training and practice on this rule, with an understanding check | Generic refresher training; longer procedures | Correct application in observed or sampled cases after the change |
| **Knowledge-based mistake** (unfamiliar situation) | Clear escalation route; "stop and ask" permission; decision aids; practice on rare scenarios; pairing for first occurrences | Blaming the person for not knowing; adding more rules for every rare case | How rare situations are handled next time; escalations raised |
| **Deliberate deviation** | Fix the reason the rule is bypassed (unworkable step, missing time or tools, conflicting targets); involve the people doing the work in redesigning it; then state and model the expectation | Punishment alone; adding a signature | Whether the deviation still occurs once the reason is removed |
| **Not yet clear** | Gather the one fact that separates the options (see question bank); interim safeguard if the risk is high | Choosing a fix now "to be safe" | — |

## Training, honestly

Training is the right answer when the evidence shows the person was not taught,
or did not understand, the specific task or rule. Then: teach that task, check
understanding by demonstration, and confirm on the job later.

It is the wrong answer when the person already knew what to do and a slip or
lapse happened, or when the procedure itself is unworkable.
