# Gotchas — people (human error review)

---

## 1. The manager's account treated as the facts

**Bad:** "The employee was trained and still made the error, so it is a
discipline issue."
**Good:** "Reported by the manager: training happened. Unknown: what the
training covered, whether it covered this rule, and whether understanding was
checked. Pull the training record and ask the employee to walk through the task."

## 2. Swapping one unproven label for another

**Bad:** Replacing "careless" with "a system failure" with no evidence either way.
**Good:** List what would show which it is, and get it.

## 3. The substitution question answered by imagination

**Bad:** "Anyone would have made this mistake."
**Good:** "Ask two colleagues how they do this step; check whether others have
had the same near miss."

## 4. Training ruled out on principle

**Bad:** "Never retrain; always fix the system."
**Good:** If the evidence shows the person was never taught the new rule,
targeted training on that rule, with an understanding check, is the fitting
countermeasure.

## 5. Stalling

**Bad:** Asking for more records after the facts already point to a clear
finding.
**Good:** State the finding, the countermeasure, and how to check it worked.

## 6. Generic error probabilities

Published figures for how often people err in different performance modes are
rough, context-bound, and in places anecdotal. Do not quote them to size a
specific risk. Use the user's own error and near-miss data.

## 7. Judging the person

Any wording that grades the individual (careless, negligent, reckless,
blameless, at fault) is out of scope. If a conduct decision is needed, point to
the organisation's HR or legal process.
