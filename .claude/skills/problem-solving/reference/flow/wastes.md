# The eight wastes — as a lens, not a checklist

Seven wastes come from the Toyota Production System (Ohno, 1988). The eighth —
not using the knowledge and ideas of the people doing the work — was added
later as lean spread beyond Toyota (see `sources.md`).

| Waste | One-line test | Shop floor / kitchen | Office / procurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defects** | Does anything have to be fixed, redone, or thrown away? | Remade dish, scrapped part | Invoice returned for a missing PO number |
| **Overproduction** | Is anything made before anyone needs it, or more than needed? | Prep for 80 covers on a 40-cover night | Reports nobody reads |
| **Waiting** | Is the item or the person idle? | Plate waiting at the pass | Request in an approver's inbox |
| **Unused knowledge** | Do the people doing the work have ideas nobody asks for? | Line cook knows a faster station layout | Buyer knows which supplier forms always fail |
| **Transport** | Is the item moved more than the work needs? | Stock moved twice between stores | Document re-keyed into a second system |
| **Inventory** | Is work or stock piling up between steps? | Full walk-in, half of it expiring | 200 unprocessed requisitions |
| **Motion** | Do people move, reach, or search more than the work needs? | Walking to a far fridge 40 times a shift | Hunting for the latest version of a file |
| **Extra processing** | Is anything done beyond what the customer needs or pays for? | Garnish nobody eats | Three signatures where one is required |

## Two traps

- **Extra processing often hides variation.** Overfilling, extra checks, and
  extra signatures are frequently a response to a process that is not under
  control. Removing them safely usually means reducing the variation first.
- **A control can look like waste.** Checks, approvals, and inspections do not
  add value for the customer but may be the only thing catching a real failure.
  Classify them as *required*, then apply evidence rule 5 before proposing any change.
